Is Your Fire Pump Bigger Than Your Building Needs?

Eng. Mohammad Yacoub Baker · Design notesم. محمد يعقوب بكر · ملاحظات تصميم (المقال باللغة الإنجليزية)

As a project owner, you may hear: “Let’s go one size up on the fire pump, just to be safe.”

It sounds reasonable. But a bigger pump does not automatically mean a safer building. It can mean a more expensive installation.

Here is what an unjustified upsize can cost you:

Higher-pressure components. If the selected pump produces excessive churn pressure, valves, fittings, and equipment may need higher pressure ratings. Adding a relief or regulating valve at the pump is not an acceptable shortcut to satisfy the maximum-pressure requirement.
Supply and suction upgrades. A higher pump flow rating changes the water-supply assessment and may require changes to suction piping or supply arrangements. Tank storage remains based on the required demand, duration, and applicable refill provisions.
More electrical capacity. A larger electric motor may require larger cables, controllers, and, where applicable, backup power capacity.

You pay for the pump, and potentially for everything that must change around it.

Before approving the upgrade, ask:

What flow and pressure does the building actually need?
Why is the additional capacity required?
What maximum pressure will the system experience?
What other costs does this selection introduce?

A justified design margin has value. An unexplained margin has a cost.

Reference: NFPA 20 (2025), §§4.6.4–4.6.5 and 4.7.7.
