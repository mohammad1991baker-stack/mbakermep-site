Case #07

Diesel Fire Pump: How Big Should the Fuel Tank Be?

Eng. Mohammad Yacoub Baker · Fire protection design notes

A common question in diesel fire pump design:

How do we determine the minimum fuel tank capacity?

According to NFPA 20:2019, Section 11.4.1.3.1, the fuel tank capacity shall be at least:

1 gal/hp (5.07 L/kW) + 5% for expansion + 5% for sump.

For example, for a 500 hp diesel engine:

500 + 25 + 25 = 550 gal minimum tank capacity.

An interesting point is found in Annex A.11.4.1.2: NFPA explains that 1 gal/hp is equivalent to 1 pint/hp per hour for 8 hours.

Important: 8 hours is an Annex explanation — not the wording of the mandatory sizing requirement.

Also, the 5% sump is not usable by the engine, so the fuel suction connection must be located above the sump volume.

Small detail. Big impact on pump-room space, tank selection, and fuel-system coordination.

These notes are for design discussion. Always apply the code edition adopted for your project and confirm with the AHJ.
