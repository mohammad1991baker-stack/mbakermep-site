Can Two Buildings Share One Fire Tank & Pump?

Eng. Mohammad Yacoub Baker · Design notesم. محمد يعقوب بكر · ملاحظات تصميم (المقال باللغة الإنجليزية)

YES — but you don’t automatically add both buildings’ demands together!

A shared fire tank and pump can serve multiple buildings, subject to hydraulic design and AHJ approval.

Example (Sprinkler + Hose Demand):

Building A: 800 GPM × 90 min = 72,000 gal
Building B: 1,100 GPM × 60 min = 66,000 gal

Governing Flow: 1,100 GPM (B)

Governing Storage: 72,000 gal (A)

What if one building needs higher flow while another needs higher pressure?

One pump may still work if its performance curve satisfies both operating points.

NFPA 20 requires at least 65% of rated pressure at 150% of rated flow for a standard centrifugal fire pump.

Always verify pressure limits, hydraulic losses, fire exposure, simultaneous demand requirements, and AHJ approval.

Key Takeaway: The governing flow, storage, and pressure may come from different buildings.

References:
NFPA 13 (2025) §1.1.3 | NFPA 20 (2025) | NFPA 20 Handbook (2013) | CSFM Interpretation 16-005

These notes are for design discussion. Always apply the code edition adopted for your project and confirm with the AHJ.
