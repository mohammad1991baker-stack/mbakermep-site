Why Calculated Sprinkler Demand Can Exceed Density × Area

Eng. Mohammad Yacoub Baker · Design notesم. محمد يعقوب بكر · ملاحظات تصميم (المقال باللغة الإنجليزية)

Density × Area is the starting point, not the final hydraulic demand.

Example: Ordinary Hazard Group 2
Density 0.20 GPM/ft² × Area 1,500 ft² = 300 GPM (theoretical)

Why the calculated demand is higher:

1. Rounding up sprinklers
1,500 ÷ 130 ft² = 11.5, so 12 sprinklers.

2. Minimum discharge per sprinkler
0.20 × 130 = 26 GPM at 21.6 psi (K-5.6)
12 × 26 = 312 GPM, already above 300 before overage.

3. Overage
Sprinklers closer to the supply see higher pressure and discharge more than 26 GPM.
In the illustrated layout, flows range from 26.0 GPM at the remote sprinkler to 31.8 GPM near the supply, for a total of about 344 GPM.

4. Hydraulic balancing
Balancing pressures at junctions can increase the calculated flow.

Note: Friction and elevation don't add flow directly. They raise the required pressure and create the pressure differences behind overage. Hose stream allowance is added on top.

Reference: NFPA 13 (2025), Chapters 19 & 28

These notes are for design discussion. Always apply the code edition adopted for your project and confirm with the AHJ.
