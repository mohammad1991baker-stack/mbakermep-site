Window Sprinklers: One Side or Both Sides?

Eng. Mohammad Yacoub Baker · Design notesم. محمد يعقوب بكر · ملاحظات تصميم (المقال باللغة الإنجليزية)

In one of our projects, glazing was used as part of a required fire separation.

A simple question came up during coordination:

Should the window sprinklers be installed on one side of the glass or on both sides?

Before proceeding, we went back to NFPA 13.

NFPA 13:2019, Section 9.3.15 addresses Sprinkler-Protected Glazing where sprinklers are used with glazing as an alternative to a required fire-rated wall or window assembly.

The code requires listed specific-application window sprinklers, unless standard spray sprinklers are specifically permitted by the building code.

More importantly:

If the assembly is required to be protected from both sides, sprinklers must be installed on both sides of the glazing.

So the real design question is not simply:

Inside or outside?

It is:

From which side can the required fire exposure occur?

For the listed arrangements shown in the figure:

  • Interior fire separation: protection can be required on both sides.
  • Exterior separation – fire originating from our building: sprinklers can be located on the inside face.
  • Exterior exposure – fire originating from an adjacent building: protection can be provided on the outside face.

The final arrangement must follow the applicable building/fire code, approved assembly, AHJ requirements, and sprinkler listing.

What about spacing?

Here we need to distinguish between NFPA requirements and sprinkler listing requirements.

For Exposure Protection, NFPA 13:2019, Section 8.7.9.7.2 limits the maximum spacing between window sprinklers to:

8 ft (2.4 m) maximum, unless listed for a greater distance.

NFPA 13:2019, Section 8.7.8.4 also requires listed window sprinklers to be positioned within 2 in. (50 mm) of the top of the window sash, while the nominal distance from the window is determined by Table 8.7.8.4.

For Sprinkler-Protected Glazing under Section 9.3.15, however, the detailed installation dimensions are controlled by the specific sprinkler listing.

For the listed arrangement shown in the second image:

6 ft (1.83 m) minimum spacing
8 ft (2.44 m) maximum spacing

If the spacing needs to be less than the listed minimum, a suitable baffle or mullion may be required between adjacent sprinklers.

And this creates an important coordination point:

The architectural mullion layout can directly affect the sprinkler layout.

Window Sprinkler design is not simply placing sprinklers beside the glass.

Fire Exposure → Required Side of Protection → Approved/Listed System → Spacing → Glass Distance → Mullions/Baffles → Hydraulic Design

A small architectural decision around glazing can completely change the fire protection arrangement.

References
NFPA 13:2019 — Section 9.3.15
NFPA 13:2019 — Section 8.7.8.4 & Table 8.7.8.4
