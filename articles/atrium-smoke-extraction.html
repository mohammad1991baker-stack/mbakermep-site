Tall Atrium Smoke Extraction: Why Exhaust at Mid-Height?

Eng. Mohammad Yacoub Baker · Design notes

The Four Seasons atrium in Guangzhou IFC runs from L70 to L100. The published engineering paper shows two smoke-extraction points at L81 and two at the top of the atrium.

So why extract at mid-height when smoke rises?

I worked through the numbers with clear assumptions (3 MW design fire, NFPA 92 plume equations):

Keeping L70–L72 clear needs about 61 m³/s. That is achievable.
Keeping everything up to L81 clear needs about 367 m³/s. Keeping every balcony clear needs about 1,900 m³/s. No fan system can do that.
A 3 MW plume may stratify at 59–89 m, below the top exhaust at roughly 110 m. Top-only fans could end up extracting mostly clean air.
At L81 the smoke is about 5× less diluted than at the top, so every m³/s extracted there removes more smoke.

The takeaway: in a tall atrium, fans don't eliminate smoke. They buy time. The real test is ASET > RSET for every floor group, and the published study reports only a 65 s margin for floors 73–92.

Hand calculations tell you where to put the exhaust and how big the problem is. CFD tells you whether the design works.
