Data Center Cooling: ΔT Decides How Much Air You Need

Eng. Mohammad Yacoub Baker · Design notesم. محمد يعقوب بكر · ملاحظات تصميم (المقال باللغة الإنجليزية)

I'm sharing this for anyone who works with HVAC, or anyone curious about how data centers are cooled.

Servers turn almost all the electricity they use into heat. Air carries that heat away, and the airflow needed comes from one simple equation:

V = Q / (1.2 × ΔT)

  • V = airflow (m³/s)
  • Q = heat load (kW)
  • ΔT = effective air-side temperature difference (K)

Example: one rack using 10 kW

At ΔT = 12 K:
V = 10 / (1.2 × 12) = 0.69 m³/s ≈ 2,500 m³/h

At ΔT = 8 K:
V = 10 / (1.2 × 8) = 1.04 m³/s

The heat is the same, but the required airflow is 50% higher.

Two different ΔTs to keep in mind:
ΔT across the servers is set mainly by the IT equipment and its fans.
ΔT across the cooling unit (supply to return) is what bypass affects. When cold air bypasses the servers and returns unused, the return gets cooler, the unit's ΔT drops, and fan energy use can rise.

The lesson: keep hot air and cold air apart. Aisle containment, blanking panels and sealed cable openings help keep the cooling unit's ΔT high.

One small note: the 1.2 factor assumes air at sea level. In high cities such as Amman, the air is lighter, so the factor needs a small correction.
