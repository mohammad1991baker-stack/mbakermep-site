Clean Agent: Don’t Forget the Underfloor Void

Eng. Mohammad Yacoub Baker · Design notesم. محمد يعقوب بكر · ملاحظات تصميم (المقال باللغة الإنجليزية)

Consider this simplified (FM-200) example:

Room area: 100 m²
Clear height above the raised floor: 3.0 m
Underfloor void depth: 0.6 m
Minimum design temperature: 20°C
Assumed design concentration: 7% (illustrative only)

Assume the room and the underfloor void form one protected enclosure, with no volume deductions and no altitude correction.

1. Calculate the protected volume

Room only:
100 × 3.0 = 300 m³

Underfloor void:
100 × 0.6 = 60 m³

Total:
300 + 60 = 360 m³

2. Calculate the agent quantity

W = (V / s) × [C / (100 − C)]

At 20°C:
s = 0.1269 + (0.0005 × 20) = 0.1369 m³/kg

Ignoring the underfloor void:
W = (300 / 0.1369) × (7 / 93) ≈ 165 kg

Including the underfloor void:
W = (360 / 0.1369) × (7 / 93) ≈ 198 kg

The overlooked void adds about 33 kg, or 20% more than the room-only calculation.

3. What happens if the void is ignored?

If only 165 kg is discharged into the full 360 m³ enclosure, the actual concentration drops to about 5.9% instead of the 7% design concentration.

Define the protected enclosure before sizing the cylinders. Then verify agent distribution, enclosure integrity, HVAC shutdown and the manufacturer's hydraulic calculations.

The 7% concentration is an example, not a universal requirement for server rooms. Final concentration and quantity depend on the hazard, the adopted standard and the listed system.
