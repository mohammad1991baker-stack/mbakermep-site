How to Read a Fire Pump Performance Curve

Eng. Mohammad Yacoub Baker · Design notesم. محمد يعقوب بكر · ملاحظات تصميم (المقال باللغة الإنجليزية)

A fire pump is not selected by GPM and pressure alone. The performance curve tells you what the pump actually delivers.

Example: NAFFCO Electric Fire Pump
Rated: 750 GPM @ 100.17 m (≈ 142 psi), 125 HP motor

Head vs. Flow
Churn (0 GPM): 107.13 m
Rated (750 GPM): 100.17 m
150% (1,125 GPM): ≈ 92 m from the curve

The 65.11 m on the datasheet is not the pump head at 150%. It is the NFPA 20 minimum (65% of rated head). The curve must pass above it.

Power vs. Flow
Duty: 75.77 kW (101.6 HP)
Peak at run-out: 100 kW (134 HP)
Peak exceeds the 125 HP nameplate rating, so motor loading must be checked against the motor’s nameplate service factor and confirmed with the manufacturer.
Efficiency vs. Flow
Duty point: 62%
Best Efficiency Point: ≈ 71% at ≈ 1,200 GPM
Selection Example
Required: 900 GPM @ 85 m
Curve at 900 GPM: ≈ 97 m, so the pump is likely suitable.
Still check suction pressure, driver loading, and maximum system pressure: churn (107 m ≈ 152 psi) plus maximum suction pressure must not exceed the pressure rating of system components.
NFPA 20 Requirements
At 150% flow: at least 65% of rated head
At churn: not more than 140% of rated head
Pump selection: the largest single system demand should not exceed 150% of rated flow. This limits the demand, not the pump’s maximum flow.

Reference: NFPA 20 (2025), Chapters 4 & 6

These notes are for design discussion. Always apply the code edition adopted for your project and confirm with the AHJ.
