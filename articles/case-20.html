Case #20

Jockey Pump Sizing — Beyond the 1% Rule

Eng. Mohammad Yacoub Baker · Fire protection design notes

A 1000 GPM fire pump does not automatically require a 10 GPM jockey pump.

The “1% of fire pump capacity” rule is a common rule of thumb, not an NFPA 20 requirement.

NFPA 20 requires:
• Sizing to restore system pressure following allowable leakage and normal pressure drops.
• Capacity not less than the normal leakage rate.
• Discharge pressure sufficient to maintain the desired system pressure.

For aboveground-only sprinkler and standpipe piping, Annex A.4.27.2.2 recommends jockey pump flow below that of a single sprinkler.

An oversized jockey pump could sustain a sprinkler discharge, preventing pressure from reaching the main fire pump’s start setting.

For extensive underground mains, assess allowable leakage using NFPA 24. The Annex also describes a sizing guideline: make up allowable leakage within 10 minutes, or provide 1 GPM, whichever is larger.

Select the jockey pump based on leakage, pressure requirements, and operating sequence—not a percentage alone.

From Chapter 14 of my upcoming fire protection book.

Reference: NFPA 20 (2025), Section 4.27.2.2–4.27.4 and Annex A.4.27.2.2. Annex guidance is explanatory, not mandatory.
