Case #01

A Small Architectural Change, a Major Engineering Impact

Eng. Mohammad Yacoub Baker · Fire protection design notes

From a real project experience, a seemingly simple decision to introduce a 2-Car Stacker into a parking area triggered a major review of the fire protection and smoke-control design.

What looked like simply adding one car vertically changed much more than the parking layout.

🚗 Conventional Parking – OH2
Density: 0.20 gpm/ft²
Design Area: 1,500 ft²
Basic sprinkler demand ≈ 300 gpm

🚗🚗 2-Car Stacker – EH2
Density: 0.40 gpm/ft²
Design Area: 2,500 ft²
Basic sprinkler demand ≈ 1,000 gpm

That means the basic sprinkler demand can increase from:

300 GPM → 1,000 GPM

More than 3 times the original demand.

This can trigger a complete review of the fire pump flow and pressure, water tank capacity, pipe sizing, sprinkler arrangement and hydraulic calculations.

And the impact does not stop with firefighting.

The CFD fire scenario can also change significantly.

For a sprinklered parking scenario:

🔥 Conventional Parking: 4 MW
🔥🔥 2-Car Stacker: 6 MW

That is a 50% increase in Design Heat Release Rate (HRR), directly affecting smoke generation, temperature, extraction requirements, jet-fan strategy and CFD results.

So, from one real project experience:

A small architectural change can affect the Hazard Classification, Fire Pump, Water Storage and the entire CFD/Smoke Control Strategy.

The lesson?

Parking configuration should never be treated as an architectural decision alone.

These notes are for design discussion. Always apply the code edition adopted for your project and confirm with the AHJ.
