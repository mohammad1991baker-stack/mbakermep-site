"Smaller and Cheaper": Why a Value-Engineered Suction Pipe Gets Rejected Twice

Eng. Mohammad Yacoub Baker · Design notesم. محمد يعقوب بكر · ملاحظات تصميم (المقال باللغة الإنجليزية)

During a submittal review for a 1,000 gpm fire pump, the contractor proposed two changes:

A 6 in Sch 40 suction pipe instead of 8 in.
A butterfly valve 20 ft (6.1 m) from the suction flange.

Both looked reasonable on paper. Both fail NFPA 20.

1. Check the suction pipe size

NFPA 20 (2025) §4.16.3.3 requires the suction pipe within 10 pipe diameters of the pump suction flange to be not smaller than the size in Table 4.28(a).

For a 1,000 gpm pump, Table 4.28(a) requires an 8 in suction pipe.

The table sizes are based on a maximum velocity of 15 ft/s at 150% of rated capacity:

Q = 1.5 × 1,000 = 1,500 gpm
v = 0.4085 × Q / d²

8 in (ID 7.981 in):
v = 0.4085 × 1,500 / 7.981² = 9.6 ft/s (2.9 m/s)

6 in (ID 6.065 in):
v = 0.4085 × 1,500 / 6.065² = 16.7 ft/s (5.1 m/s)

The 6 in pipe is below the minimum size in the table, and its velocity exceeds the 15 ft/s basis. Rejected.

Note on editions: in earlier editions (e.g., NFPA 20 2019, §4.14.3.4), the 15 ft/s limit at 150% was a mandatory clause. In the 2025 edition, the mandatory requirement is the minimum pipe size from Table 4.28(a), and 15 ft/s appears as the basis of the table.

2. Check the valve

No control valve other than a listed OS&Y valve shall be installed in the suction pipe within 50 ft (15.3 m) of the pump suction flange (§4.16.5.4).

The butterfly valve is rejected. Use an OS&Y gate valve, or move the butterfly valve more than 50 ft (15.3 m) away.

3. One more check

At 150% of rated flow, the suction pressure shall not drop below 0 psi (§4.16.3.1), or −3 psi where the supply is a suction tank at or above the pump centerline (§4.16.3.2).

The pump is selected at 100%, but the suction pipe is checked at 150%. A suction pipe that works at rated flow can still fail at the flow the pump must deliver.

Adapted from Chapter 14 (Example 14-5) of my Fire Fighting Systems – Book.

Reference: NFPA 20 (2025), §4.16.3, §4.16.5.4, Table 4.28(a).

These notes are for design discussion. Always apply the code edition adopted for your project and confirm with the AHJ.
