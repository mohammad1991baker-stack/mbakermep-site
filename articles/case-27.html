Case #27

Dry Sprinkler or Dry System?

Eng. Mohammad Yacoub Baker · Fire protection design notes

A freezing area does not automatically require a separate dry pipe system.

Consider a heated warehouse with a wet system and an adjacent outdoor canopy requiring sprinkler protection.

A separate dry pipe system introduces a dry valve, air supply, and applicable water-delivery checks.

Where the layout and listing permit, dry-type sprinklers can instead extend from the heated space into the freezing area while the supply piping remains wet.

NFPA 13 explicitly addresses dry sprinklers connected to wet pipe systems.

Before selecting them, verify:

The sprinkler’s listed coverage and orientation.
The minimum exposed barrel length inside the heated space.
The manufacturer’s adjusted K-factor for hydraulic calculations.
Protection of water-filled piping from freezing.

For a small freezing area, selecting the right sprinkler may avoid the complexity and maintenance of an additional system.

A dry sprinkler does not make a dry system.

From Chapter 10 of my Fire Fighting Systems – Student Book.

Reference: NFPA 13 (2019), Sections 15.3.1–15.3.2 and 27.2.4.10.3, as shown in the attached excerpts; manufacturer’s listed installation instructions.
