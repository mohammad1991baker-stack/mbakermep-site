A 100 psi Pump Can Overpressure a 175 psi System

Eng. Mohammad Yacoub Baker · Design notesم. محمد يعقوب بكر · ملاحظات تصميم (المقال باللغة الإنجليزية)

The pump’s rated pressure is not the maximum pressure the system may experience.

Example:
• Rated pump pressure: 100 psi (6.9 bar).
• Net churn pressure: 120 psi (8.3 bar).
• Maximum static suction pressure: 65 psi (4.5 bar).

At the same elevation:
120 + 65 = 185 psi (12.8 bar).

This exceeds the rating of 175 psi (12.1 bar) components.

NFPA 20 §4.7.7.1 requires net churn pressure plus maximum static suction pressure, adjusted for elevation, to remain within component pressure ratings.

Relief valves or pressure-regulating devices in the fire pump installation cannot be used to satisfy this requirement (§4.7.7.2). Compliant variable-speed arrangements are addressed separately in §4.7.7.3.

For diesel-driven pumps, §4.20.1.2 requires an additional check:
1.21 × net rated churn pressure + maximum static suction pressure, adjusted for elevation.

If this exceeds component ratings, a main relief valve is required. This does not replace the basic maximum-pressure check.

Verify both the hydraulic demand point and maximum system pressure.

Reference: NFPA 20 (2025), §§4.7.7 and 4.20.1.2.
