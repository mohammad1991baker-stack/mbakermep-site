The Largest Pump Demand Does Not Always Size the Fire Tank

Eng. Mohammad Yacoub Baker · Design notesم. محمد يعقوب بكر · ملاحظات تصميم (المقال باللغة الإنجليزية)

It is common to size the fire water tank from the scenario that needs the most pressure or flow. The governing scenario is actually the one with the largest flow × duration.

Consider this example from a mixed-use building with one fire pump and one tank:

Standpipes: 750 gpm × 30 min = 22,500 gal
Light hazard offices: 343 gpm × 30 min = 10,295 gal
Parking garage: 580 gpm × 60 min = 34,786 gal
Archive: 314 gpm × 60 min = 18,819 gal
Dry ramp: 640 gpm × 60 min = 38,400 gal

The standpipes govern the pump: 750 gpm at 173 psi, so a 750 gpm × 175 psi pump is selected.

The tank is governed by the dry ramp: 38,400 gal (about 145 m³).

If the tank were sized from the pump scenario (22,500 gal), it would be about 40% too small.

Two more checks from the same example:

The fill means shall refill the tank within 8 hours: 38,400 / 480 min ≈ 80 gpm.

The maximum system pressure is churn plus maximum suction: 201.2 + 5.2 ≈ 206 psi. Every component exposed to it must be rated for it, not only the sprinklers.

Size the pump from the highest pressure demand, and size the tank from the largest flow × duration. They are often different scenarios.

Adapted from Chapter 25 (Example 25-5) of my Fire Fighting Systems – Student Book.

References: NFPA 13 (2025) Table 19.2.3.1.2; NFPA 14 (2024); NFPA 20 (2025); NFPA 22 §14.4.2.
