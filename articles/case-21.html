Dry Pipe Systems: Increase the Area, Not the Density

Eng. Mohammad Yacoub Baker · Design notesم. محمد يعقوب بكر · ملاحظات تصميم (المقال باللغة الإنجليزية)

A 30% increase in design area is not interchangeable with a 30% increase in density.

Under the density/area method, NFPA 13 requires the operating area to increase by 30% for dry pipe and double-interlock preaction systems, without changing the density.

Example, considering this adjustment alone:
Base design: 0.20 gpm/ft² over 1500 ft²
Adjusted design: 0.20 gpm/ft² over 1950 ft²

Using 0.26 gpm/ft² over the original 1500 ft² does not satisfy the requirement, even though density × area gives the same 390 gpm.

Why the difference matters:
At 130 ft² per sprinkler, the design area grows from 12 to 15 operating sprinklers.
The area extends further along the branch lines (about 46 ft to 53 ft), adding friction loss and overage.

The actual demand must be calculated for the larger arrangement, and it will typically exceed the simple product.

From Chapter 10 of my fire protection book.

Reference: NFPA 13 (2025), Section 19.2.3.2.6
